Current Divider

Fundamentals

A Current divider is a passive linear circuit (series) that outputs a fraciton of the total current (current from source). If two or more impedances are in parallel, the current that enters the combination will be split between them in inverse proportion to their impedances (according to Ohm's law). It also follows that if the impedances have the same value, the current is split equally.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_divider (information source, Wikipedia)

Uses

Current divider circuits are widely used to split current between multiple parallel components to meet the specific requirements of electrical elements.

Formulas Rearrangements

The formula can be rearranged to find the current or the resistance. The rearrangements are as follows:

  • Ix = (Isource * RTotal) / (Rx * RTotal)
  • Isource = ((Rx + RTotal) * Ix) / RTotal
  • Rx = (RTotal * (Isource - Ix)) / Ix
  • RTotal = (Rx * Ix) / (ITotal - Ix)
